*************
What is MUSE?
*************

The `Multi-slit Solar Explorer (MUSE) <https://muse.lmsal.com/>`__ is a NASA `Medium-Class Explorers (MIDEX) <https://explorers.gsfc.nasa.gov/midex.html>`__ satellite, managed by the `Lockheed Martin Corporation Advanced Technology Center (LM ATC) <https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/capabilities/space/atc.html>`__ and led by Principal Investigator (PI) Dr. Bart De Pontieu of the `Lockheed Martin Solar & Astrophysics Laboratory (LMSAL) <https://lmsal.com/>`__.

Building on IRIS, MUSE studies the Sun's corona instead of the chromosphere: with its instruments and numerical modeling, it targets the processes that heat the multi-million degree corona, accelerate the solar wind and drive the solar activity (flares and CMEs) that impacts Earth.

MUSE combines simultaneous sub-arcsecond coronal spectroscopy (0.4") along 35 slits with imaging (0.33"), and has 30 to 100 times the coverage and cadence of previous spectrographs, thanks to a telemetry rate over 30 times higher.

The MUSE payload consists of:

* The Multi-slit Spectrograph: collects line profiles in bright coronal lines (Fe IX 171 Å, Fe XV 284 Å, Fe XIX 108 Å, Fe XXI 108 Å), covering a large temperature range (0.7-12 MK) at the highest angular (0.4") and temporal resolution (1 s exposure time) ever achieved. Its field of view (FOV) is 151 x 170 arcsec (raster x slit direction; the default raster in this package, 35 slits x 11 steps x 0.4", spans 154 arcsec).
* The Context Imager: based on the Hi-C rocket, collects high-resolution (0.33") images (Fe XII 195 Å and He II 304 Å) over a large field of view, showing transition-region and coronal morphology and motions. Its FOV is 580 x 290 arcsec.

MUSE addresses all of NASA's Heliophysics Science Goals, enables advances in all Key Science Goals of the `Solar and Space Physics Decadal Survey <https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/13060/solar-and-space-physics-a-science-for-a-technological-society>`__, and fills a gap in the capabilities of the Heliophysics System Observatory and of instruments such as DKIST, EUVST, Parker Solar Probe and Solar Orbiter.
